Understanding PCOS and Nutrition

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS/PCOD) is a hormonal condition that affects menstrual cycles, fertility, weight, skin, and energy levels. While there's no single "cure," research consistently shows that the right nutrition can significantly improve symptoms. Key nutritional strategies include managing blood sugar and insulin response, reducing chronic inflammation through anti-inflammatory foods, supporting hormonal balance with adequate nutrients, and achieving a healthy weight to improve cycle regularity.

Insulin Resistance

The right food combinations and meal timing help your body use insulin more effectively — often the root cause of PCOS symptoms.

Hormonal Balance

Specific nutrients like omega-3s, zinc, vitamin D, and inositol support hormonal pathways when included in the right amounts.

Anti-Inflammatory Diet

Chronic low-grade inflammation worsens PCOS. Anti-inflammatory Indian foods like turmeric, ginger, and whole grains can help.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a PCOS dietitian do?

add
A PCOS dietitian creates personalized nutrition plans that address the hormonal and metabolic aspects of PCOS. This includes managing insulin resistance through food choices, supporting hormonal balance, addressing inflammation, and creating sustainable eating patterns that work with your body.

Can I follow an Indian diet with PCOS?

add
Yes. Many traditional Indian foods are excellent for PCOS management. Whole grains like bajra, jowar, and ragi, combined with adequate protein from dal, paneer, and eggs, along with anti-inflammatory spices like turmeric and cinnamon, can be very effective.

Can nutrition help with PCOS-related weight gain?

add
Nutrition is one of the most important factors in managing PCOS-related weight. The right diet addresses insulin resistance (a root cause of PCOS weight gain), reduces inflammation, and supports hormonal balance. Many clients see significant improvements in weight, energy, and cycle regularity.

Do I need to stop eating rice with PCOS?

add
Not necessarily. The key with PCOS is managing blood sugar spikes. Dt. Disha may recommend specific portion sizes, pairing rice with protein and fiber, choosing certain rice varieties, or adjusting meal timing rather than eliminating rice entirely.
